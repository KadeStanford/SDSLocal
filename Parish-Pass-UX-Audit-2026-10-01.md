# Parish Pass: independent UX and visual-design audit

**Date:** October 1, 2026. **Workspace:** `F:\BusinessApp`. **Method:** source-based expert walkthrough of the current working tree, including existing uncommitted changes; base commit `79af559`. This is not a measured usability study or a deployed-build certification.

Parish Pass has a credible customer browsing experience and unusually thoughtful transaction recovery. Its largest weaknesses are the continuity of customer commitments and the business owner's path from a paid or preview account to an approved, usable listing. The visual foundation is coherent; the most consequential problems are navigation, state accuracy, and recovery rather than cosmetic styling.

## Readiness verdict and scores

**Customer readiness: suitable for supervised testing of registration, discovery, follows, events, rewards, and service requests. Not ready for unrestricted use of the entire advertised journey.** Registration recovery needs a clearer verification state. Appointment retrieval and the signed-in event calendar can lose the user's practical route back to a commitment. Pickup ordering and appointments have deliberate pilot/environment restrictions; their presence in source does not establish production availability. Web rewards also misrepresent points programs.

**Business-owner readiness: suitable for assisted setup and testing of established businesses; needs work before broad self-service onboarding.** An owner can create a draft and edit much of it sensibly. However, skipping categories creates a mobile publishing dead end, completion checks can remain stale after successful saves, and review rejection feedback is absent from the mobile workflow. Requiring payment before approval increases the importance of explaining the requirements and review process clearly.

The scores below include account creation and empty accounts. They assess native customer/owner journeys primarily, with deductions for the separately implemented web destinations reached through sharing and account flows. They are judgments of the code-supported experience, not observed completion rates.

| Dimension | Maximum | Customer | Business owner | Main deductions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Task completion and workflow simplicity | 30 | 21 | 15 | Customer appointments and saved event commitments lack dependable retrieval; owner category recovery and publishing completion can block progress. |
| Navigation and discoverability | 25 | 18 | 15 | Following is inside Rewards; commitments are split across destinations; business Account moves with business capabilities; web/mobile handoffs are incomplete. |
| Clarity and information hierarchy | 20 | 15 | 11 | Verification next steps, calendar scope, two brands, competing owner checklists, and approval outcomes require interpretation. |
| Forms, feedback, and recovery | 15 | 10 | 10 | Good transaction safeguards offset verification recovery gaps, contradictory optional-name validation, hours interruption loss, and generic deletion errors. |
| Visual consistency and accessibility | 10 | 7 | 7 | Strong shared tokens and controls; repeated nested surfaces and dense secondary controls need rendering checks. Accessibility semantics are promising but unverified in assistive technology. |
| **Total** | **100** | **71** | **58** | **Code-based expert assessment.** |

Confidence is **high** for the specific route, validation, query, and state-handling findings; **moderate** for discoverability and reading burden; **limited** for actual layout, keyboard behavior, screen-reader operation, and perceived visual quality. A score change of a few points would be less meaningful than resolving the high-severity findings.

## Scope and evidence rules

I reconstructed both journeys from account creation, then examined their established states. I traced Expo routes, conditional screens inside large components, the Next web application, public support/legal content, shared schemas and business logic, relevant SQL migrations, and transaction/recovery handlers. I read applicable operational instructions. I did not use previous chats, audits, design approvals, or galleries as evidence.

No application code was changed. No accounts, database writes, emails, purchases, external messages, or transactions were performed. Metro was not started or changed. There was no phone testing or rendered browser walkthrough. This report is the only artifact created for the audit.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Confirmed:** directly established by implementation. The reported experience is reconstructed, not observed on a device.
- **Inferred:** a likely usability consequence or layout concern derived from the implementation.
- **Requires rendering/device/external verification:** the source does not establish the outcome sufficiently.

Severity reflects the consequence for the intended task, including conditional branches. **High** means a core task can become blocked, hard to recover, or materially misleading. **Medium** means significant avoidable effort or uncertainty. **Low** means limited friction or polish. “Needs substantial redesign” classifies the experience; it does not imply that a whole application rewrite is necessary.

## Journey reconstruction from account creation

### New customer

1. **Signed out → Account → Create account.** Guest Home and Events are available first; registration is not forced just to browse. Account initially offers sign-in, with a Create account alternative. Email/password registration asks for a display name, email, and a password with explicit requirements. Google is configuration dependent; Apple is platform dependent. The display-name input appears required by its label but permits an empty value. [Account registration](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:1148>), [signup validation](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:427>).
2. **Submit → email verification or an immediate session.** If Supabase returns no session, the form displays “Check your email” in place. There is no distinct pending-confirmation screen with resend and change-email actions. Email-code authentication offers a second route, with a numeric code entry and autofill, but the user must understand that it is an alternative to the visible password workflow. Invalid callback links lead back to sign-in. No delivery or redirect configuration was exercised. [Pending verification](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:437>), [callback recovery](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/auth/callback.tsx:60>).
3. **Authenticated → Home or the action that requested authentication.** Pending follow/rewards/RSVP/request intents have an explicit return mechanism. There is no mandatory long customer onboarding questionnaire. A customer can immediately search, open a business, follow it, or join a rewards program. Optional profile/location editing is separate. [Intent continuation](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:219>), [public sign-in gate](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/public-business-page.tsx:1863>).
4. **First useful action → Home → business → Follow.** This is about two content transitions after authentication, without another long form. The business page exposes relevant actions according to business type and capability. Follows are later found within Rewards → Following. Empty rewards explain joining from a business page; empty Following explains following a business, although it calls Home “Discover.” [Business actions](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/public-business-page.tsx:1169>), [Following empty state](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:990>).
5. **Events → By date / All events → detail → RSVP.** The detail separates time/place, descriptions, group size, attendance/waitlist status, and reminder choices. RSVP can be one decision plus group-size adjustment. However, signed-in By date queries followed businesses, while guest discovery can show all public events. Saving an RSVP/reminder does not independently add that event to the calendar query. [Calendar data](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:459>), [RSVP result](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:1392>).
6. **Optional commerce branches.** Pickup has five explicit preparation stages—menu, cart, pickup, contact, review—followed by provider payment and order status. Appointment booking has service, time, contact, and review, followed by payment where required and appointment detail. Service requests have draft and review stages, then a confirmation/history path. These stages are mostly justified, but booking repeatedly advances one day and lacks a customer history destination. [Pickup review](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/pickup/pickup-checkout-steps.tsx:208>), [booking stages](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/book-appointment.tsx:283>), [request review/recovery](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/service-request.tsx:267>).
7. **Established use.** Rewards and rotating codes, pickup history, service-request history, attended-event reviews, alerts, and account settings are reachable. Appointment history is the conspicuous omission. A reminder alert's event detail is also a simpler surface than the full event detail, which adds a navigation detour to RSVP controls. Deletion has a careful impact/confirmation flow, but blocked deletion is reported generically. [Account activity](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:878>), [alert event detail](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/notification.tsx:528>), [deletion flow](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/account-data-panel.tsx:49>).

### New business owner

1. **Account creation and verification use the same customer account flow.** A brand-new account has no business membership; business mode is not an independent registration type. “List your business” and Account → Create a business establish the owner path. An authentication intent can carry that purpose through sign-in. The eventual creation success refreshes membership, selects business mode, and opens the new workspace. [Owner continuation](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:647>), [mode/access](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/providers/app-mode-provider.tsx:37>).
2. **Create a business → plan gate.** All mobile creation entry points share a gate. With billing enabled, the owner chooses a plan and possibly monthly/yearly billing, completes the native store purchase, waits for server entitlement, then continues. With billing disabled, an explicit preview continuation is available. An existing full account is stopped with a slot-capacity explanation. The copy explains automatic renewal, no trial, billing beginning at confirmation, and publication requiring approval. [Shared gate](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-creation-gate.tsx:8>), [plan branches](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/listing-plans.tsx:214>), [purchase consequences](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/listing-plans.tsx:427>).
3. **Four-step draft creation.** Business identity → How you serve → Location or area → Review and create. The owner provides a name, business type, optional categories/description, a service model, and relevant location/coverage. The page address is generated with customization and collision recovery. Local draft saving and an idempotent creation request support interruption. Hours, contact details, and branding images are deferred. [Step definitions](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:153>), [local draft](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:282>), [create payload](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:488>).
4. **Created draft → workspace → finish publication requirements.** Creation is not the end of onboarding. The owner must complete seven backend requirements: description of at least 20 characters, a category, contact, suitable location/coverage or mobile stop, seven-day hours, optimized logo, and optimized cover. A first offering is encouraged by a separate setup tracker, but is not one of these seven publication requirements. This entails several editor round trips; photos may require a picker/upload for each role. A category omitted during draft creation cannot be added in the mobile profile editor. [Requirements](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260919000500_resumable_business_onboarding.sql:200>), [hub tracker](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:845>), [publishing checklist](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:6043>).
5. **Checklist complete → Submit for SDS review → await review → published or draft again.** The button depends on loaded readiness rather than always refreshing after each save. Mobile shows status but does not fetch/render the stored rejection feedback. Edits while pending can reset the backend to draft. The owner is not given a complete status story: what was rejected, what edits do to a submission, and how to resubmit. [Submit UI](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:6075>), [review feedback/reset](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260915000900_review_and_offering_management.sql:299>).
6. **Established business operations.** The hub groups customer experience, operations, and marketing/visibility. Menus/services, photos, contact, hours, location/mobile stops, events, updates, rewards, staff, reviews, analytics, and sharing are nested destinations. Orders, staff scanning, and service-business appointments/requests also have operational tabs. Booking setup separates services, schedule, rules, and team/resources; intake forms have question editing and customer preview. Provider payment setup is resumable. These are substantial and useful capabilities, but are too dependent on accurate access and readiness state to tolerate the identified ambiguities. [Hub](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-hub.tsx:148>), [booking setup](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/appointment-workspace.tsx:788>), [intake builder](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/request-form-builder.tsx:65>).
7. **Interruption, plan restriction, expiry, and recovery.** Creation drafts are stored on the device, not advertised as cross-device drafts. Many editors warn about unsaved work, but hours are excluded from the workspace's dirty-state check. Feature gates explain upgrade/recheck options and preserve selected existing-operation recovery routes. Backend logic expressly permits earned-reward redemption and existing appointment rescheduling for previously approved listings suspended for billing; this is a strength, not an expiry defect. [Dirty-state cases](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:980>), [feature gate](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-feature-gate.tsx:53>), [existing obligations](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20261001000100_existing_obligation_recovery.sql:2>).

## Prioritized findings

### F1. A skipped category cannot be repaired in the mobile publishing flow

**High · Business owner · Functional task blocker. Confirmed implementation; abandonment risk inferred.**

**Task/path:** Create an account → Create a business → skip optional Categories, or encounter their load failure → create draft → Publishing checklist → Edit “At least one category.”

Categories are labeled optional during creation, and a load-error message promises they can be added later. Publishing requires a category. The checklist sends the owner to Profile, whose editor and save payload have no category control. The mobile source only writes business categories during creation. The owner therefore reaches a required action without the means to perform it. Web settings can edit categories, but mobile does not provide that recovery handoff.

**Evidence:** [Optional categories](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:684>), [load-error promise](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:347>), [backend requirement](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260919000500_resumable_business_onboarding.sql:201>), [category destination](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/lib/business-workspace-config.ts:24>), [mobile profile editor](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:3377>), [save payload](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:1205>), [web category editor](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/account/businesses/[id]/settings/business-details-form.tsx:153>).

**Improve:** Add an accessible category editor after creation; route the checklist directly to it; preserve primary-category order and the existing five-category limit. Keep creation optional only if later completion truly works. **Scope: small to medium**, involving editor UI, persistence, and readiness refresh.

### F2. Successful setup saves can leave publishing disabled

**High · Business owner · Functional state/feedback defect. Confirmed on the specified sequence.**

**Task/path:** Draft workspace → complete the last missing requirement in Contact, Profile, Location, or Hours → save → return to Publishing without leaving/reloading the workspace.

Readiness is assigned in the workspace load. These successful saves update local content and a notice but do not fetch readiness again. Section changes are local rather than a new route focus. The checklist and Submit button can continue using the previous incomplete result. A focus refresh, pull refresh, or a later action that reloads the workspace can mask the problem; it is not universal on every completion order.

**Evidence:** [Readiness load](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:685>), [focus refresh](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:710>), [hours save](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:1184>), [profile save](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:1205>), [section navigation](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:922>), [disabled submission](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:6077>).

**Improve:** Refresh authoritative readiness after relevant saves and on entering Publishing, with a visible checking state. Keep the submit handler's server validation. **Scope: small to medium.**

### F3. Customers have no dependable appointment history destination

**High · Customer · Navigation/task-continuity gap. Confirmed absence of a direct list in the audited routes; difficulty recovering inferred.**

**Task/path:** Business → Book appointment → confirmation/detail → leave → return later to view, reschedule, or cancel.

The booking flow stores access to a particular appointment and replaces the route with its detail. The detail needs an appointment ID; signed-in users can recover access when they already have that ID. There is no customer appointment-list route, Account activity entry, or booking section in the event calendar. Secure storage is keyed by business/appointment rather than exposed as a browseable history. Notifications do not establish a general appointment-history solution in the audited source.

**Evidence:** [Post-booking route](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/book-appointment.tsx:216>), [detail access requirement](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/appointment.tsx:157>), [stored access](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/lib/appointment-commerce.ts:41>), [Account activity](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:878>), [customer tabs](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/app-tabs.tsx:74>), [alert entity types](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/notification.tsx:82>).

**Improve:** Add My appointments with upcoming/past states, reliable authenticated retrieval, cancellation/rescheduling links, and a confirmation link back to that list. Define same-device guest recovery separately. **Scope: medium to large**, including a customer read model and navigation.

### F4. The signed-in event calendar does not independently include RSVPs or saved events

**High · Customer · Functional selection/navigation mismatch. Confirmed queries; user expectation inferred.**

**Task/path:** Events → All events → RSVP or set a reminder for an unfollowed business → later open Events → By date.

RSVP persistence creates an event save/reminder. Calendar data nevertheless comes from upcoming events of followed businesses, then groups that array by day. Event-save IDs are used for reminder indicators, not to add events to the calendar. A new account with no follows can have a saved commitment and an empty By date view. The controls say “By date” and “All events,” which suggests a display change more than a change in eligibility. Guest and signed-in behavior also differ.

**Evidence:** [Control labels](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/events-view-switch.tsx:23>), [saves/follows reads](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:391>), [follow-only event query](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:480>), [date grouping](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/rewards.tsx:724>), [RSVP saves a reminder](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260929000100_fix_rsvp_result_type.sql:72>).

**Improve:** Include going/waitlisted/saved events independently of follows, and name the scopes explicitly. Consider “My plans” versus “Browse events,” with a true date/list toggle within each scope. **Scope: medium.**

### F5. Optional read failures can erase the useful page; access uncertainty can erase owner navigation

**High · Both roles · Recovery/resilience problem. Confirmed conditional behavior; outage frequency unknown.**

**Task/path:** Home → open a business, or owner opens workspace, while a supplementary data read fails. Separately, an owner re-enters the app while the membership check times out.

Several pages load independent datasets together and treat any error as a page-level failure. A loyalty/photo/follow or other supplementary query can prevent showing the core business. Public business error UI has no direct Retry action. Home has the same broad first-error coupling, although ratings/pickup reads already demonstrate more graceful degradation. The mode provider also interprets an error or timeout as no business access and switches to customer mode, so an availability problem looks like lost membership.

**Evidence:** [Home first-error coupling](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/explore.tsx:178>), [public business first-error coupling](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/public-business-page.tsx:431>), [public error surface](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/public-business-page.tsx:805>), [owner workspace reads](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:559>), [membership uncertainty](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/providers/app-mode-provider.tsx:44>).

**Improve:** Separate core identity reads from supplementary sections, add section-level retry, retain safe previously loaded data, and distinguish access unknown from access denied. Keep server authorization intact. **Scope: medium**, across data/state composition.

### F6. Account verification lacks a complete recovery screen

**High · New customer and new owner · Onboarding usability gap. Confirmed controls; delivery success untested.**

**Task/path:** Account → Create account → successful signup without session → wait for email, discover an incorrect address, or open an expired link.

Signup remains on the form with a check-email notice. There is no pending confirmation screen with resend, change email, expected next step, and recheck/continue. The email-code component handles code entry well but provides no resend action. An invalid callback says to request a new link, then takes the user to sign-in rather than directly to that action. This leaves users to reconstruct the authentication options, particularly when they started with an owner intent.

**Evidence:** [Signup notice](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:437>), [code alternative](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:1403>), [code verification component](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/email-code-verification.tsx:1>), [expired callback](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/auth/callback.tsx:60>).

**Improve:** Introduce one pending verification screen preserving email and intended destination, with resend/cooldown, change email, and explicit expired-link recovery. Align password, code, and provider choices around that flow. **Scope: medium.** This is not a claim that hosted email or deep links fail.

### F7. Publication requirements and review outcomes do not tell one consistent story

**High · Business owner · Onboarding/status clarity gap. Confirmed implementation; confusion inferred.**

**Task/path:** Plan → draft creation → setup tracker → publishing checklist → submit → rejection or edit while pending.

The short creation flow is sensible, but Categories and Description being optional there obscures what remains mandatory for publication. The hub tracker uses a nonempty description instead of the backend's 20-character rule and adds a first offering that publishing does not require. Mobile reads no `review_feedback` despite the backend storing rejection/resubmission reasons. Publishing collapses nonactive/nonpending status to Draft and says “SDS review,” with no clear process, expected next step, or relevant help route. Pending edits can return a submission to draft.

**Evidence:** [Tracker rules](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:845>), [publication rules](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260919000500_resumable_business_onboarding.sql:256>), [workspace business selection](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:582>), [status/submit copy](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:6031>), [rejection feedback](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260915000900_review_and_offering_management.sql:299>), [pending edit reset](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260915000900_review_and_offering_management.sql:340>), [web displays feedback](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/account/businesses/[id]/settings/page.tsx:142>).

**Improve:** Use authoritative publication checks everywhere, distinguish required publication items from recommended improvements, preview requirements before payment/creation, show rejection reasons and resubmit actions, and explain edits while pending. Provide a status-specific help path. **Scope: medium**, combining state, copy, and onboarding structure.

### F8. Hours can look saved when no rows exist, and unsaved edits can disappear

**Medium · Business owner · Functional default/interruption defect. Confirmed.**

**Task/path:** New draft → Hours → observe seven closed days → leave without changing; or change hours → workspace Back without saving.

Creation writes no hours. The editor synthesizes seven closed days, compares the synthesized initial and current drafts, and labels its disabled button “Hours saved” when unchanged. That label describes local equality rather than persistence. Leaving an unchanged all-closed schedule cannot save its required seven rows. For changed schedules, the parent dirty-state guard has no hours case, and the editor has no parent dirty callback. The normal workspace back path can discard these edits without the safeguard used by other editors.

**Evidence:** [No hours on creation](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/business-new.tsx:500>), [synthetic defaults](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace-panels.tsx:704>), [dirty comparison](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace-panels.tsx:544>), [“Hours saved” button](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace-panels.tsx:657>), [parent guard](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-workspace.tsx:980>).

**Improve:** Track missing persisted rows separately from unchanged values, make initial saving explicit, propagate dirty state to navigation, and retain or confirm discard on departure. **Scope: small to medium.**

### F9. Web rewards treat points programs as visit-stamp programs

**Medium · Customer · Functional misinformation. Confirmed.**

**Task/path:** Shared business web page → join a spend-points program → web Rewards → open card.

The public web profile describes points programs correctly. The web wallet calls the legacy stamp-only RPC, has no program type/points fields, and renders visits and stamp progress. Card detail similarly uses the legacy membership RPC and visit labels. The native wallet already uses the richer v2 model. For points memberships, earned value and progress cannot be represented accurately by this web presentation.

**Evidence:** [Web wallet read/model](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/rewards/page.tsx:6>), [visits rendering](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/rewards/page.tsx:58>), [web detail read](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/rewards/[membershipId]/page.tsx:38>), [detail visits label](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/rewards/[membershipId]/page.tsx:67>), [web points promise](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/b/[slug]/page.tsx:552>), [legacy versus v2 wallet](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20261001000100_existing_obligation_recovery.sql:13>).

**Improve:** Use the points-aware read model and type-aware balance/history components on web; maintain a deliberate legacy fallback. **Scope: medium.**

### F10. Sharing leads into a differently branded and less capable product surface

**Medium · Both roles · Cross-surface continuity problem. Confirmed surfaces; trust/abandonment effects inferred.**

**Task/path:** Owner → Share page / QR poster → recipient opens `/b/[slug]` in web; or browser owner starts Create business.

Mobile uses Parish Pass, while the web brand and metadata repeatedly say SDS Local. The generated printable QR poster also says “Open SDS Local.” Shared URLs use the configured public HTTPS base, so actual domain/deep-link behavior remains deployment dependent. In the Next business profile there is no equivalent ordering, appointment, quote-request, reviews/reporting/blocking flow or explicit contextual handoff for those tasks. Browser business creation tells an unsubscribed owner to subscribe in the native app and return, but provides no concrete install/open bridge. Web event detail also lacks native RSVP controls. These are capability boundaries, not proof that the native functions fail.

**Evidence:** [Public URL construction](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/lib/share-links.ts:28>), [poster brand](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-qr-poster.tsx:111>), [web metadata](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/layout.tsx:9>), [web business composition](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/b/[slug]/page.tsx:1>), [web event composition](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/events/[businessSlug]/[eventSlug]/page.tsx:1>), [browser subscription gate](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/account/businesses/new/page.tsx:21>).

**Improve:** Align user-visible branding, define the intended browser capability set, and supply contextual open-app/install/return actions for unsupported tasks while preserving the business/event context. **Scope: medium to large**, depending on whether web gains parity or a deliberate handoff.

### F11. Customer support and policy links are difficult to discover

**Medium · Customer, also owner · Navigation/labeling gap. Confirmed placement; legal adequacy not assessed.**

**Task/path:** New account wants terms/privacy; established customer needs help with login, a request, or deletion → Account.

Account has Profile, Notifications, Privacy & Safety, Sign-in methods, and Account & data, but no direct Help/Support or policy destination. Privacy & Safety contains blocked-business management rather than the privacy policy. Configured terms/privacy/support links are exposed through business subscription screens. Static public pages exist, so this is chiefly a discoverability issue, not an absence of support content.

**Evidence:** [Account destinations](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:833>), [subscription legal links](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/listing-plans.tsx:476>), [subscription support](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/listing-plans.tsx:559>), [public support/privacy/deletion content](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/public-info/content.mjs:31>).

**Improve:** Add direct help and policy links to Account and appropriate registration/transaction states; distinguish platform help from contacting the business. Rename or expand Privacy & Safety. **Scope: small to medium.**

### F12. “Display name (optional)” becomes mandatory when saving a profile

**Medium · Customer and owner account · Validation inconsistency. Confirmed.**

**Task/path:** Register without a display name → Account → Profile → add city/state → Save.

Signup allows an empty name, and the profile label calls it optional. The profile schema requires at least two characters. A user making an unrelated profile edit must supply a name contrary to the label. The registration label itself omits the optional qualifier.

**Evidence:** [Signup permits blank](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:432>), [optional profile label](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:964>), [profile parse](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/account.tsx:475>), [required schema](<F:/BusinessApp/packages/validation/src/index.ts:180>).

**Improve:** Make the schema and labels agree, accepting blank names if they are truly optional; validate nonblank values consistently across signup, code/provider accounts, and profile editing. **Scope: small.**

### F13. Deletion safeguards do not explain an obligation that blocks deletion

**Medium · Both roles · Recovery feedback defect. Confirmed masking; specific deployed blockers untested.**

**Task/path:** Account → Account & data → Review deletion impact → confirm while an unresolved appointment/payment prevents deletion.

The flow carefully explains business deletion and separate store billing. But backend appointment rules can reject deletion until upcoming appointments/outstanding payments are resolved. The Edge handler maps failures to generic deletion messages, and the mobile catch replaces them with “safely try again.” A deterministic blocker is therefore presented as something retrying might fix. Impact preview focuses on affected businesses rather than the outstanding task needing resolution.

**Evidence:** [Appointment blocker](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/migrations/20260927000400_square_sandbox_appointments.sql:1438>), [handler maps conflict](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/functions/delete-account/index.ts:123>), [generic server copy](<F:/BusinessApp/supabase/functions/_shared/account-deletion.ts:53>), [generic client copy](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/account-data-panel.tsx:112>).

**Improve:** Return safe, structured blocker categories in impact preview/error responses, and link to the appointment/order/payment action or support needed to resolve them. Preserve existing confirmation safeguards. **Scope: medium.**

### F14. Appointment date selection and contact entry add avoidable effort

### F15. Navigation and terminology shift with role and business type

**Medium · Both roles, especially owners · Discoverability issue. Confirmed labels/conditions; confusion inferred.**

**Task/path:** Customer wants followed businesses → Rewards → Following; service owner wants Account after enabling service operations → header icon instead of the former bottom tab; retail owner opens offerings → Services.

Following lives inside Rewards, although following a business does not require rewards. Customer Home is called Discover in some explanatory copy. When service tabs are present, Account is removed from the bottom tabs and is reached through app chrome. Offering headings and item/category terminology call nonfood businesses “Services,” even for retail/general businesses. ### F16. The visual system is coherent, but stacked grouping can flatten hierarchy

**Low · Both roles · Inferred layout concern and aesthetic judgment, not a confirmed rendered defect.**

**Task/path:** Open a business page or a dense owner editor, then scan for the primary action among headings, overview rows, cards, subcards, and sheets.

## Visual and accessibility assessment

**Confirmed design foundation.** The shared mobile palette uses a warm light background (`#F6F5F0`), green accent (`#176B4D`), and explicit dark-mode colors rather than a simple color inversion. Body text is 16/24; titles 30/36; metadata 14/20; captions 13/18. Spacing follows a small consistent scale. Customer and merchant surfaces use restrained borders, rounded corners, and distinct status treatments. Image components have meaningful fallbacks rather than requiring every listing to have imagery. [Tokens](<F:/BusinessApp/packages/design-tokens/src/index.ts:22>), [typography](<F:/BusinessApp/packages/design-tokens/src/index.ts:133>), [business identity images](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/business-identity-row.tsx:1>).

**Controls and forms.** Shared inputs use 16-point text, a 52-point minimum height, filled backgrounds, and a focus/error underline; multiline inputs start at 108. Merchant buttons expose disabled/busy states and labels. Tab controls expose selected state; collapsible FlowSection content is hidden from the accessibility tree when closed. These are real strengths. The optional-name mismatch and hours “saved” state undermine this otherwise consistent control language. [Inputs](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/app-text-input.tsx:38>), [merchant actions/tabs](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/merchant-ui.tsx:81>), [collapsible semantics](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/mobile/src/components/flow-layout.tsx:47>).

**Composition judgment.** Customer identity, time/place facts, and transaction stages are generally separated clearly. Dense inventory editors use filtering and detail tabs to avoid one enormous form. The weaker compositions are long setup/status pages and repeated nested card sections. It is plausible that compact captions, horizontal choices, photo controls, and multiple secondary actions become hard to scan at large text sizes; this needs rendering, not a claim of observed clipping. F16 describes a targeted refinement rather than a new visual identity.

**Web.** Web uses related green/warm colors, 46-pixel main buttons, grid forms with 18-pixel gaps, rounded inputs, and an explicit focus border/shadow. Below 780 pixels, major multi-column grids become single-column. This is a sensible responsive foundation, but the branding differs and owner forms expose much more content at once. Source evidence does not prove visual excellence at intermediate widths or with long content. [Web controls/forms](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/globals.css:84>), [form focus](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/globals.css:235>), [responsive rules](<F:/BusinessApp/apps/web/src/app/globals.css:2150>).

**Accessibility limits.** I found no basis to declare the product accessible or inaccessible as a whole. The source contains many appropriate labels, radio/tab states, generous targets, selected-state text, live regions, code autofill, and manual alternatives to scanning. Verify modal focus/return, native tab semantics, reading order, filled-input identification, keyboard avoidance, image alternatives, dynamic type, screen-reader announcement of errors/status, color contrast under custom business colors, and reduced motion. Map, camera, system picker, provider payment, and OS permission flows require platform testing. There is no blanket compliance verdict here.

## Coverage limitations and verification still needed

The inventory covers the relevant implemented customer and owner routes, nested editors/sheets, and significant conditions found in the current source. Some presentation details are intentionally grouped—for example day/time pickers and repeated destructive confirmations—rather than counted as separate copies of the same control. Missing customer appointment retrieval and missing post-creation mobile categories are explicitly inventoried; they are not silently marked covered by another page.

The following remain unevaluated as runtime outcomes:

- **Rendered layouts:** small/intermediate widths, landscape, large text, keyboard overlap, safe areas, long names/descriptions, large inventories, actual nested-sheet sizing, and scroll/gesture interactions. F16 is a hypothesis to check, not a screenshot-based defect.
- **Assistive technology:** VoiceOver/TalkBack, browser keyboard traversal, modal focus/return, system pickers, live-region timing, reduced motion and custom-color contrast. Source semantics were examined; no compliance result is claimed.
- **Authentication:** real signup/delivery, configured confirmation redirects, universal links, provider account selection/linking, password-recovery expiry, biometric/session persistence, and cross-device returns. No email was sent.
- **Commerce and subscriptions:** enabled product catalogs, live entitlement values, native store dialogs, provider onboarding/checkout, real order/appointment/payment/refund completion, external reimbursement, and web/native return behavior. No purchase or database change occurred.
- **Device capabilities:** cameras/torch/QR accuracy, printing/system share, map gestures/geocoding, foreground/background location, notification permission settings and actual push/reminder delivery.
- **Deployed backend state:** migrations and defaults establish intended requirements, not which migrations/rollout flags are active in a particular hosted environment. Latest source includes pilot gates and obligation recovery; deployed parity must be verified independently.
- **Content/operations:** real business coverage, menu completeness, category taxonomy quality, review turnaround, support responsiveness, notification relevance, and actual moderation outcomes. No claim is made that real users failed, abandoned, or completed a task.
- **Scope exclusions:** platform-administrator consoles, developer diagnostics as standalone tools, legal-policy adequacy, security/compliance certification, and previous audit/design material. Owner/customer states that depend on admin decisions were traced through their status/feedback consequences.

Before broader customer testing, verify F3/F4/F6 and the shared-link/points boundaries, then exercise a representative empty account and returning customer. Before broad self-service owner onboarding, verify category recovery, final-requirement save → submission, review rejection/resubmission, hours interruption, and paid-plan/preview/expired states. Use local or sanctioned test fixtures and configured pilots; the audit requires no real-user emails or purchases to justify these priorities.
